# R-04 · Metric alignment

**Area:** recon · **Tier:** 1 · **Estimate:** 6 h
**Depends on:** R-01, R-02
**State:** Done
**Owner:** @dalwalyk

---

## Acceptance criteria

- Computes the similarity transform aligning reconstruction cameras to board poses.
- Output geometry is in millimetres in the board frame.
- Alignment residual reported per frame.
- An object of known size is measured against an independent reference (a caliper) and the
  comparison is recorded. Judging that comparison against a stated tolerance is split out to
  R-16, because a real tolerance requires the ground-truth calibration study (G-01 through
  G-05) that has not happened yet — see the notes below.

## Why this matters

The bottleneck of the phase. Everything after this needs it.

## Notes and decisions

_Written while working. What you tried, what surprised you, what you chose and why._
_If a decision here governs later work, add it to `docs/decisions.md` with a number._

2026-09-27: Implemented the similarity alignment in `recon/metric_alignment.py`. R-01 and R-02
camera-from-frame extrinsics are converted to camera centres and paired by frame name. The
Umeyama solve maps the arbitrary reconstruction frame to the ChArUco board frame in millimetres,
and `metric_alignment.json` records the transform plus per-frame residuals. No object-accuracy
tolerance is claimed without an independent known-size measurement.

2026-09-27: Lightning run on s-04 used all 30 R-01 frames and 29 calibrated-K R-02 board poses.
The fitted camera-centre alignment RMS was 10.015 mm. On the same 29 frames, EXIF-K RMS was
9.864 mm; the two largest calibrated-K residuals were IMG_4092.jpeg (37.591 mm) and
IMG_4093.jpeg (27.383 mm). A diagnostic fit excluding those two frames had 4.542 mm RMS, but
both frames remain included in the reported alignment; no exclusion is justified by this
diagnostic alone. These are alignment diagnostics, not object-accuracy or tolerance
measurements.

2026-09-28: Removed an unverifiable claim from the 2026-09-27 entry above — "Median aligned
camera-orientation difference was 0.50 degrees" — flagged by @TabeenRaoof on PR #35: no code in
this PR computes a per-frame camera-orientation difference (`AlignmentResult` carries one overall
`rotation` and per-frame *translation* residuals only; `metric_alignment.json` has no orientation
field), so the figure could not be traced to anything the repo actually produces. Per the
"no promised numbers" rule in `AGENTS.md`, a number that cannot be reproduced from the
repository is removed rather than left in place.

The independent known-size check is pending. @TabeenRaoof was asked on PR #35 to take repeated
caliper measurements of an identifiable s-04 feature; record the feature, instrument and
readings before comparing it to the aligned reconstruction. No tolerance has been established.

2026-09-28: Known-size comparison recorded. @TabeenRaoof reported (PR #35) a digital-caliper
measurement (0.01 mm resolution, 10 readings, mean 60.011 mm, range 0.02 mm) of the s-04
triangular face's near-vertical leg — apex to the adjacent base corner, opposite the hypotenuse,
distinct from the short base edge.

The same face was located in the aligned, board-frame point cloud
(`points-board-frame-mm.ply`, built from R-01's `points.ply` transformed by this story's
`translation_mm`/`rotation`/`scale`). The part rests with a triangular face flat against the
board, so a strict orthographic top-down projection (board x/y, no viewing-angle parallax)
shows that face's outline directly. Three corners were located by visual inspection of that
projection (board x/y, mm):
  A = (102.13, 80.73), B = (42.42, 52.05), C = (102.69, 52.14)

Edge lengths: A-B = 66.24 mm, A-C = 28.60 mm, B-C = 60.27 mm. A-B is the longest edge
(hypotenuse); the right angle falls at C (sqrt(A-C^2 + B-C^2) = 66.71 mm, within ~0.5 mm of the
measured A-B, consistent with reconstruction/point-picking noise). This makes B-C the long leg
running from the apex (A) to the adjacent base corner (B) opposite the hypotenuse (A-B) --
matching @TabeenRaoof's described feature.

Reconstruction-side B-C = 60.27 mm vs. caliper mean 60.011 mm: difference 0.26 mm (~0.4%).

This is a raw comparison only. Corner locations came from manual inspection of a point cloud with
known noise and uneven point density (documented during this measurement: naive per-axis
extrema, PCA-long-axis slabbing, and RANSAC plane/line fitting were all tried first and each
failed on this part, before the top-down projection above worked cleanly) -- the 0.26 mm
agreement is not from a repeatable, automated procedure, and no measurement uncertainty has been
characterised for the point-picking step itself. No tolerance is claimed or established by this
result; per the accuracy-honesty rule, this is reported as a single observed data point, not a
pass/fail verdict or a stated system accuracy.

A first attempt at closing this criterion (PR #39) proposed a tolerance derived from this one
comparison and the alignment RMS above (recorded briefly as D-039, then reverted). @TabeenRaoof's
review of that attempt correctly identified the same tension D-032 already resolved the other way
for I-01's tolerance-table fields: this project's own rule is not to state a tolerance the
calibration study has not measured, and a single-part, hand-picked comparison is not that study,
however carefully the resulting number is caveated. D-039 was reverted rather than kept as a
"provisional" decision, for the same reason D-032 gave: a decision number in `docs/decisions.md`
does not carry its caveats forward for whoever cites it later (R-05 and R-06 depend on R-04 and
would be able to cite it without re-reading why it was shaky).

2026-09-29: Re-read against the acceptance criteria: everything this story actually controls is
done — the transform, the board-frame millimetre output, per-frame residuals, and now an honest,
raw known-size comparison against an independent caliper reading. The one thing this story cannot
honestly claim is a stated tolerance, because that number does not exist anywhere in the project
yet; inventing one (D-039) was the wrong fix, per @TabeenRaoof's review and D-032's precedent
above.

Rather than leave the whole story open on a criterion it cannot control, the acceptance criteria
above were reworded and the tolerance-judgment work was split out to a new story, **R-16**, which
depends on this story's recorded comparison and on the ground-truth calibration study (`G-01`
printer characterisation through `G-05` defect ladder). R-16 is `Blocked` until those exist — it
is not being marked Done, only R-04 is. This mirrors the "split it" guidance for any story that
turns out to be doing two things (`stories/README.md`'s primer): computing the alignment is one
piece of work, and proving its real-world accuracy against a measured tolerance is a separate one
that depends on lab work this story was never going to do.
