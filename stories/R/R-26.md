## Why this exists

R-21 sets the cut height at the top of the densest layer within `DEFAULT_MAX_BOARD_LAYER_MM`
(10 mm) of the fitted board plane, and it histograms every point in the footprint, the part's
included. A part whose top is a large flat surface less than about 10 mm above the board makes
exactly such a layer, so the cut lands just above it and the part is removed with no error. In
the review's probe a 40 × 40 × 6 mm plate lost 1523 of its 1600 top-surface points (#180).
`fuse_run` uses the same search, so the service would fuse a mesh with the part largely missing.
Every golden part is taller than 10 mm, which is why this never showed up.

The error's own remedy doesn't work either. When the search fails, it tells the user to pass an
explicit `height_threshold_mm`, but all three callers run the search before applying the
override, so the call still raises.

## Acceptance criteria

- A flat part lower than `DEFAULT_MAX_BOARD_LAYER_MM` survives segmentation. How the board layer
  is told apart from the part is the owner's call: for example, counting only bins contiguous
  with the plane's own layer, or histogramming only points away from the part. Record it.
- An implausibly small kept share of the part raises an error instead of returning a near-empty
  segmentation. The limit is a declared parameter, its default stated as an assumption.
- An explicit `height_threshold_mm` skips the layer search in `segment_run`, `fuse_run` and the
  confidence sweep, so the error's advice works.
- The five golden objects are rerun on R-23's alignment. For each, record the threshold beside
  R-21's table, and whether the board is still excluded: the per-object check R-21's third
  criterion asked for and never recorded.

## Tests

- a thin flat plate (for example 40 × 40 × 6 mm) on a synthetic board keeps its top surface;
- the kept-share error fires on a planted near-empty result;
- an explicit override succeeds on input where the search alone would raise, in each of the three
  callers.

The golden rerun and the board-exclusion check are measurements, recorded with figures.

## Notes and decisions

- Closes #180 (`Fixes #180`).
- R-21's notes saw the flat-part case and recorded it as not a bug. Add a dated pointer there to
  this story.
- The fourth criterion also settles the review's other R-21 finding. R-21's third criterion (board
  still excluded, per object) was never recorded, and a probe on the 9 October runs, made before
  R-23 changed the alignment, suggested the board may not be excluded on s-02 and s-04. If the
  rerun confirms that, say so plainly; don't adjust thresholds to make it pass.
- **Round 1 (superseded):** the layer-top histogram was first restricted to a margin band near
  the footprint's outer edge. Review on #241 (@TabeenRaoof), against real golden data, found two
  real problems: a part could still reach *into* the margin without covering the footprint edge
  to edge (#180 wasn't fixed, just narrowed), and on real data the smaller margin-restricted
  sample broke R-17's own raised-layer detection, lowering the cut and letting more board back
  in as "part" on s-02/s-03. Superseded by round 2, below.
- **How the board layer is told apart from the part (criterion 1, round 2):** the layer-top
  search now runs per `layer_search_cell_mm`-sized spatial cell (default 10 mm) over the *whole*
  footprint, then takes the **median** across every cell's own local top as the consensus
  (@TabeenRaoof's suggestion on #241's review). A board layer is spread over many cells; a part
  occupies a compact minority of them, so the median can't be moved by it regardless of where on
  the footprint the part sits — and the search is over the same full point population the plane
  fit already uses, so a sparse real board's upper fringe keeps the density it always had. A
  cell with too few points, or whose own search never thins out, contributes no local top rather
  than a guess; too few valid cells overall still raises. Cell size, the per-cell minimum point
  count, and the minimum number of valid cells are all bare assumptions (D-033), not measured.
- **Kept-share floor (criterion 2, round 2):** an absolute floor alone missed #180's own case
  (77 of 1600 points survived — comfortably over an absolute 50) and a companion probe (stray
  points elsewhere satisfying the absolute floor while the real part was lost). Added
  `min_kept_fraction`: the kept count must be at least this share of a loose, height-only pool
  (above `min_clearance_mm`, independent of whatever threshold was actually used) — if most of
  what's above the noise floor didn't survive the chosen cut, the cut likely ran through the
  part, not past it. `segment_part`'s own default stays 0/disabled (general-purpose primitive,
  exercised at every scale including small synthetic tests unrelated to this check);
  `segment_run`/`fuse_run` default both floors to `DEFAULT_MIN_KEPT_POINTS` (50) and
  `DEFAULT_MIN_KEPT_FRACTION` (0.5, by analogy with A-04's `min_core_vertices`);
  `confidence_sweep_run`'s diagnostic sweep deliberately explores small-keep regions across
  thresholds, so both stay at their permissive defaults there.
- **Round 2 (superseded):** the median across cells also failed real golden data, a second time.
  Review on #241's second pass found the kept-share floor hard-failed s-01/s-02 even at a
  *correct* cut (the loose, whole-footprint pool is inflated by real board fringe above
  `min_clearance_mm`), and the median itself can't tell a part from a raised layer covering
  anywhere up to about half the board's cells — TabeenRaoof's own synthetic sweep showed a
  raised layer at 25% or 40% cell coverage still gets wrongly kept as if it were the part, while
  only 60%+ coverage gets correctly excluded. Golden parts measured at only 3-8% of cells, so
  this gap matters in the direction that costs real parts, not just in theory. Superseded by
  round 3, below.
- **Side-wall connectivity (criterion 1, round 3):** confirmed with the user, including a known
  risk, before implementing (see below). Per-cell local tops (unchanged from round 2) are now
  additionally classified: a cell whose own search never thins out is `connected` directly (no
  internal gap at all is itself wall-like). Remaining cells above the 10th-percentile baseline
  by more than `elevation_margin_mm` are `elevated`, grouped into spatially-connected clusters.
  Each cluster is pooled with its immediate neighbours and checked for points at heights
  strictly between the baseline and the cluster's own height — `min_wall_points` there (a
  deliberately low, generous bar) marks the cluster `connected` (excluded entirely from the
  consensus, as the part); none marks it `floating` (possible legitimate board evidence, R-17's
  case, included as before). The median is now taken only over cells never classified
  `connected`.
  **Known, accepted residual risk:** connectivity can only protect a part whose wall was
  actually reconstructed. #180's own 6 mm plate may never have a reconstructed wall at that
  thickness, in which case there is no signal left to tell it apart from a floating layer, and
  it is not protected. Demonstrated directly as a test
  (`test_residual_risk_a_large_wall_less_part_with_real_occlusion_is_not_protected`), not left
  undemonstrated; this is a known limitation of this approach, not a bug to fix in this pass.
- **Kept-share floor (criterion 2, round 3):** redefined from a generic height-only pool to
  `BoardSurface.part_candidate_point_count` — the points the layer-top search itself identified
  as `connected` (probably the part), from the same per-cell/cluster analysis above. The share
  check is silently skipped when that count is `None` (the override path, or nothing was
  identified) rather than treated as a 0% share; `min_kept_points` is still the floor then.
- **This pass covers criteria 1-3 and their tests only.** Criterion 4 (the five-golden-object
  rerun on R-23's alignment, recording the threshold and board-exclusion per object) needs the
  golden fixture set, which this implementation pass doesn't have direct access to —
  @TabeenRaoof's #241 review has run it twice already against the two superseded algorithms and
  offered to rerun again; that rerun, once it lands against round 3, is what the completion
  record and D-038 close-out should be based on. State stays `Claimed`, not `Done`.
