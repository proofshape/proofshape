## Why this exists

R-06 keeps points more than 2 mm above z = 0, which assumes R-04 placed the board surface exactly
at z = 0. R-17 measured where it actually is, by fitting a plane to board points around each part.
On all five golden objects the real board sits 1.6–5.4 mm below z = 0, with tilt ≤ 0.9°. So the
cut lands 3.6–7.4 mm above the board and removes the bottom of every part. On s-04 that's 7.4 mm of
a 30 mm part, a quarter of it. R-04 aligns camera centres, not the board plane, so an offset of a
few millimetres is expected rather than a bug in R-04.

## Acceptance criteria

- The height threshold is applied relative to the board surface measured from the reconstruction
  (a robust plane fit on near-board points around the part), not z = 0. The measured offset and
  tilt are written to `part_segmentation.json`. A fit that fails or is implausible is an explicit
  error, never a silent fallback to z = 0. That covers too few board points, or a tilt beyond a
  named, overridable limit.
- The threshold accounts for how noisy the reconstructed board is, not just a fixed 2 mm. On
  s-01/s-02, VGGT reconstructs a weaker second board layer 4–6 mm above the real surface. Today's
  misplaced cut happens to sit near it; moving the cut down to the real board without handling it
  would let that layer in. Much of it is low-confidence, which R-20 addresses.
- R-17's segmentation check is rerun on all five golden objects. Record per object that the base
  is kept and the board is still excluded.

## Tests

Synthetic clouds cover:
- A board plane offset by a few millimetres both above and below z = 0, and tilted, with the
  part's base recovered in each case.
- A raised, noisy second layer that must stay excluded.
- A fit with too few points, which must raise.

The real-data rerun is a manual check, the same exception as R-17.

## Notes and decisions

2026-10-09 (when written up, from R-17's findings): an alternative is to fix this at the source, by
refining R-04's alignment so VGGT's board points land on z = 0. That would also change the scale,
which R-22 is investigating. The owner chooses, and records which and why.

R-17's measured offsets of the real board below z = 0:

| s-01 | s-02 | s-03 | s-04 | s-05 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.2 mm | 1.6 mm | 2.8 mm | 5.4 mm | 3.3 mm |

`fuse_run` applies `segment_part`, so this also reaches R-07's fused meshes (see R-20).

2026-10-11: **Chose to measure in R-06, not fix at the source in R-04** (D-044). R-22 is mid-flight
on a different R-04/R-01 scale question at the same time; touching R-04's alignment now would move
the ground under that measurement. The threshold also needs the plane fit's own residual noise
(see below), so R-06 has to fit a plane regardless of what R-04 does — fixing R-04 wouldn't remove
that need, only add a second place computing a related number.

**Design: `measure_board_surface` in `recon/part_segmentation.py`.** Candidate board points are
everything inside the footprint within 15 mm of the assumed z=0 (wide enough for any offset this
project has measured, narrow enough to exclude a hand-sized part). A robust plane fit (median
start, then least-squares with iterative MAD-sigma clipping, deterministic — no RANSAC) gives the
offset and tilt; a tilt beyond 2° raises rather than silently accepting a bad fit, same for fewer
than 1000 candidate or inlier points. Offset and tilt both land in `part_segmentation.json`'s new
`board_surface` block.

**The threshold is no longer a fixed 2 mm.** A height-above-plane histogram (0.5 mm bins, out to
10 mm) finds the *top* of the highest dense bin — not just the first sparse one, so a raised second
layer separated from the real board by a gap still gets caught — and the threshold clears it, floor
2 mm. This directly targets R-17's s-01/s-02 finding (a weaker second board layer 4–6 mm up): moving
the cut straight down to the fitted plane with the old fixed 2 mm margin would have let it through.
If the dense layer never thins out within the 10 mm search window, that's an explicit error (covers
a genuinely very thick/noisy board, and a part that's itself thin and flat across the whole
footprint) rather than a guess.

**One accidental find while writing the synthetic "raised layer" test:** a part modeled as a single
flat slab of points at one height (rather than spread across a height range, as a real 3D object's
visible surface would be) looks exactly like a secondary board layer to the density check — same
single-bin spike. Not a bug in the production code; it meant the "part base recovered" tests needed
a lone point rather than a dense synthetic cluster to test what they were actually testing (height
measured relative to the fitted plane, not z=0), leaving the dense-cluster scenario to the
dedicated "raised layer must be excluded" test where it belongs.

**`fuse_run` (`recon/tsdf_fusion.py`).** Fits the same surface from its own valid-depth pixels only
(finite, >0) — an invalid pixel unprojects to the camera centre, not a board point, and would
corrupt the fit if included. The voxel grid's z bounds now follow the fitted (possibly tilted)
plane's footprint corners instead of assuming the board sits at z=0, so the grid itself doesn't
repeat R-06's old assumption and clip the base before fusion even starts.

**Bug found and fixed in the same pass, not a pre-existing R-21 scope item but directly adjacent:**
the original `fuse_run` called `segment_part` on every pixel's unprojected point, including pixels
with invalid (non-finite) depth — `segment_part`'s finiteness check rejects the *whole* array if
any single point is non-finite, so one bad pixel anywhere in a frame would have crashed the entire
fusion run. `vggt_runner.py` already anticipates non-finite depth elsewhere in this codebase, so
this wasn't hypothetical. Fixed by only passing finite-depth points through `segment_part` and
defaulting everything else to "not kept" — `fuse_tsdf` already ignores invalid-depth pixels
independently of this mask, so their keep value was never load-bearing.

**Real-data rerun, all five golden objects** (`python -m recon.part_segmentation` against each
object's real R-01 + R-04 output; `fuse_run` smoke-tested end to end on s-04 only — the full R-18
mesh-quality rerun belongs to R-20, which this story unblocks rather than duplicates):

| | s-01 | s-02 | s-03 | s-04 | s-05 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Measured offset (mm) | 1.9 | 0.9 | 3.2 | 5.4 | 3.2 |
| Measured tilt (deg) | 0.38 | 0.53 | 0.23 | 0.78 | 0.47 |
| New height threshold (mm) | 6.0 | 4.5 | 3.5 | 2.5 | 3.5 |
| R-17's old cut above the real board (mm) | 4.2 | 3.6 | 4.8 | 7.4 | 5.3 |
| Kept points / total | 99,263 / 5,279,456 | 110,728 / 6,091,680 | 373,038 / 5,482,512 | 121,446 / 6,091,680 | 133,811 / 5,888,624 |

Offsets are within ~0.7 mm of R-17's (which used a one-off script against a slightly different
candidate selection); s-01/s-02 come out lower than R-17's because their raised second layer pulls
a naive estimate up, and this fit's outlier rejection is specifically designed to reject that layer
rather than average it in. All five thresholds exceed R-17's measured second-layer height where one
was found (s-01: 6.0 > 4–6, s-02: 4.5 > 4–6 at the low end), and drop below R-17's old, over-cut
threshold on three of five objects. **Correction (2026-10-12, caught in code review on the
progress-log close-out PR):** s-01 and s-02 are both exceptions, not just s-01 as first written
here — s-02's new threshold (4.5) exceeds R-17's old cut (3.6) the same way s-01's does (6.0 vs
4.2). In both cases the old cut happened to sit *under* the true board's noisy top rather than
above it.

Visual check (equal-aspect, `-z` as height, same convention as R-17): on all five objects the part
base now extends down to the board, including s-04's triangular prism, which previously lost 7.4 mm
(a quarter of its 30 mm height) and now visibly reaches the x-axis. The large amount of stray
clutter well above each part (loosely sheet-like structures, scattered background points) is
unchanged by this story — that's R-17/R-20's low-confidence-point finding, not a height-cut problem,
and R-20 addresses it directly. **This is the manual visual pass/fail R-17 used as precedent, and
it's @TabeenRaoof's call to make, not mine** — figures and measurements above are for that review,
not a substitute for it.

`fuse_run` smoke-tested on s-04's real data end to end (not the full R-18 rerun, which is R-20's):
completed in well under a minute, 75,404 vertices / 105,658 faces, `board_surface` block present
and matching `segment_run`'s on the same object.
