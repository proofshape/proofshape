## Why this exists

R-06 keeps points more than 2 mm above z = 0, which assumes R-04 placed the board surface exactly
at z = 0. R-17 measured where it actually is, by fitting a plane to board points around each part.
On all five golden objects the real board sits 1.6–5.4 mm below z = 0, with tilt ≤ 0.9°. So the
cut lands 3.6–7.4 mm above the board and removes the bottom of every part. On s-04 that's 7.4 mm of
a 30 mm part, a quarter of it. R-04 aligns camera centres, not the board plane, so an offset of a
few millimetres is expected rather than a bug in R-04.

## Acceptance criteria

- The height threshold is applied relative to the board surface measured from the reconstruction
  (a robust plane fit on near-board points around the part), not z = 0. The measured offset and
  tilt are written to `part_segmentation.json`. A fit that fails or is implausible is an explicit
  error, never a silent fallback to z = 0. That covers too few board points, or a tilt beyond a
  named, overridable limit.
- The threshold accounts for how noisy the reconstructed board is, not just a fixed 2 mm. On
  s-01/s-02, VGGT reconstructs a weaker second board layer 4–6 mm above the real surface. Today's
  misplaced cut happens to sit near it; moving the cut down to the real board without handling it
  would let that layer in. Much of it is low-confidence, which R-20 addresses.
- R-17's segmentation check is rerun on all five golden objects. Record per object that the base
  is kept and the board is still excluded.

## Tests

Synthetic clouds cover:
- A board plane offset by a few millimetres both above and below z = 0, and tilted, with the
  part's base recovered in each case.
- A raised, noisy second layer that must stay excluded.
- A fit with too few points, which must raise.

The real-data rerun is a manual check, the same exception as R-17.
