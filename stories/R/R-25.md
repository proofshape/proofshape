## Why this exists

R-09 counts a frame as observing a vertex when the vertex's depth is within 5 mm of the depth that
frame measured at its pixel. Nothing checks that the surface faces the camera. So surface hidden
less than 5 mm behind a seen surface, such as the back of a thin wall, is labelled **Observed**.
R-10's σ downgrade can't catch it, because its residuals are a consistent offset with almost no
spread (#178). Observed is the only label that may pass or fail a part (D-005), so this is the
hard rule's own mechanism letting unseen geometry through.

Two neighbours of the same gate:

- σ is computed only over residuals that already passed the 5 mm gate, so it can never exceed
  5 mm (#185). A frame that disagrees by more is dropped as "occluded" instead of widening σ.
- The service reports `observed_fraction` from R-09's labels, before R-10's downgrade, as a share
  of faces. It then serves the downgraded model, and the frozen contract promises surface area
  (#179).

## Acceptance criteria

- A frame only observes a vertex whose surface faces that frame's camera: `n · (C − v) > 0`, with
  vertex normals from the mesh as `recon/next_view.py` already computes them. The depth tolerance
  is a declared parameter exposed on the CLI, its default stated as an assumption, not a measured
  value. Whether it is tied to the fusion voxel size is the owner's call; record it.
- σ no longer truncates silently at the occlusion tolerance. Either it is computed over a wider
  residual window than the gate, or each vertex records how many observations the gate dropped
  and `uncertainty.json` reports it. Record which and why, and document the behaviour where σ is
  defined.
- The service's `observed_fraction` comes from the final, post-downgrade labels of the model it
  serves, weighted by triangle area as the contract says.
- `provenance_run` and `uncertainty_run` are rerun on the five golden objects, on R-23's
  alignment. The per-object change in Observed vertices and `observed_fraction` is recorded beside
  R-24's numbers, so the effect is measured, not assumed.

## Tests

Written with the logic, one or more per criterion:

- a vertex 3 mm behind a measured surface is not observed;
- a front-facing and a back-facing vertex at the same depth are told apart;
- σ (or the drop count) when one frame disagrees beyond the gate;
- `observed_fraction` on a mesh with unequal triangle areas and a downgraded vertex, checked
  against the labels in the GLB the service serves.

The golden rerun is a measurement, not a test.

## Notes and decisions

- **Depth tolerance:** keep the default at a configurable 5 mm and do **not** tie it to the fusion
  voxel size. The voxel size is a reconstruction sampling choice, while this gate asks whether a
  mesh vertex agrees with an actually measured frame depth. Until calibration provides a measured
  tolerance, 5 mm remains an explicit assumption exposed on the CLI rather than a derived accuracy
  claim.
- **σ truncation:** keep σ over the residuals that passed the provenance depth/occlusion gate and
  record, per vertex, how many otherwise-eligible frame samples that gate rejected; aggregate those
  counts in `uncertainty.json`. Widening σ's residual window would let samples already classified
  as occluded influence uncertainty. Reporting the dropped counts makes the truncation explicit
  without weakening the occlusion rule.
- **Open-mesh normal sign:** local face winding is made consistent first. Closed meshes use signed
  volume for the outward global sign. Open meshes use only trusted, in-bounds, depth-consistent
  vertex/frame pairs computed without the facing gate to choose between the two global signs.
  Hidden back faces and unrelated cameras therefore cannot flip the sign based on part size or
  camera height; with no usable evidence the normals stay zero and the hard rule remains
  conservative.

unmeasured assumption, this result is recorded for later calibration rather than treated as a
new accuracy claim.
