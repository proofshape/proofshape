## Why this exists

R-06's acceptance criteria originally included "visually verified on every golden object; hands
and background excluded." That criterion needs real `points.ply`/`metric_alignment.json` for all
five golden objects (s-01..s-05) — the output of R-01 (VGGT) and R-04 (metric alignment) actually
run on real photographs — to look at. R-06 was implemented and fully unit-tested against synthetic
data on a machine with no GPU/torch and no local `fixtures/data/recon_runs`, so that look-and-check
step could not happen in the same session. Rather than merge R-06 with an unmet criterion, or block
it indefinitely on GPU access that has nothing to do with whether the segmentation logic itself is
correct, the criterion is split out here — the same move R-04 made for its known-size tolerance
judgment (see `docs/decisions.md` and `stories/R-04.md`'s notes for that precedent).

## Acceptance criteria

- For each of the five golden objects, `recon/part_segmentation.py`'s `segment_run` (or its CLI,
  `python -m recon.part_segmentation`) is run against that object's real R-01 + R-04 output.
- Each resulting `segmented_points.ply` is opened in a viewer (MeshLab, CloudCompare, or
  equivalent) and visually confirmed: the part is intact, and the board pattern, table and any
  hands/background clutter are excluded.
- Per-object result (pass, or what was wrong) is recorded in this story's notes below. A failure
  on a specific object is not automatically a defect in the segmentation logic — it may mean
  `height_threshold_mm` or the default footprint need adjusting for that object's geometry, both
  of which are already configurable parameters on `segment_part`/`segment_run`.

## Tests

This is the manual-check exception from `AGENTS.md`'s testing rule: "visually verified" is
inherently a human judgment on real photographs of real objects, not something an automated test
asserts. `recon/part_segmentation.py`'s own logic (the height/footprint predicate, the file I/O,
the alignment transform) is already fully covered by synthetic-data unit tests written alongside
it in R-06 (`tests/test_part_segmentation.py`) — this story is only the real-data look-and-check
that those tests cannot stand in for.

**Visual review by @TabeenRaoof: none of the five passes.** Stray points around every part, soft
edges, and sizes that look wrong apply to all five, not just the one example examined most closely
(s-05). The measurements behind that verdict, per object:

| Object | Board excluded? | Stray points outside the part | Part's base cut off |
|---|---|---|---|
| s-01 padlock, lighting A | No: a weaker second board layer 4–6 mm above the real board shows through as a grid | 19.3% | 4.2 mm |
| s-02 padlock, lighting B | No: same layer, plus depth-edge "skirts" from the padlock down to the board | 19.0% | 3.6 mm |
| s-03 rounded object | Yes | 11.5% | 4.8 mm |
| s-04 triangular prism | Yes | 29.3% | 7.4 mm (a quarter of its 30 mm height) |
| s-05 rectangular prism | Yes | 17.7% | 5.3 mm |

What causes each, and who owns the fix:

- **Stray points are low-confidence pixels.** R-01 keeps every pixel VGGT returns (`--min-confidence
  0.0`). About three-quarters of pixels sit at VGGT's confidence floor (s-05: median 1.0, 75th
  percentile 1.003). As a diagnostic only, not a chosen threshold, dropping points below 1.5 cuts
  stray points to 1.1–2.3% on all five without changing the part's measured size. It also removes
  most of s-01's raised board layer, and part of s-02's. R-07's fusion doesn't read VGGT's confidence
  at all. → **R-20**.
- **The base cut comes from where R-06 assumes the board is.** After R-04 the real board surface
  (a plane fitted to board points around each part) sits 1.6–5.4 mm *below* z = 0 on all five, with
  tilt ≤ 0.9°. So R-06's "2 mm above z = 0" lands 3.6–7.4 mm above the board. R-04 fits camera
  centres, not the board plane, so a few millimetres of offset is expected. → **R-21**.
- **Parts reconstruct smaller than their CAD.** This could only be checked for the two objects that
  have one. Heights: s-04 28.2 vs 30 mm (−5.8%), s-05 57.2 vs 60 mm (−4.6%). Mid-height
  cross-sections fit the CAD outline best at 0.88 (s-04) and 0.83 (s-05) of nominal size.
  Horizontal shrinks far more than vertical, so R-04's single scale factor can't be the whole
  cause. Running R-03 on s-04/s-05 shows VGGT's focal length is 3–5% longer than the
  board-calibrated one: right direction, too small to be the whole story. → **R-22**.

These are R-17 *findings*, not fixes. This story is a look-and-check with no code, and each cause
above is real work in its own right. Caveats on the numbers: they're measured on the raw point cloud
before fusion; sizes are against the CAD's nominal dimensions, not calipers (G-03 hasn't happened);
the measurement code was a one-off script, which R-22 replaces with a tested one. Stray points mean
segmented points outside the largest connected cluster of occupied 2 mm voxels (≥ 3 points each),
counting only points more than 2 mm above the fitted board.

**Relation to R-18.** R-18 attributed the failed fused meshes on s-03/s-04/s-05 to `segment_part`'s
height/footprint parameters. Segmentation here isolates those three parts cleanly, apart from the
base cut and the stray points, which R-21 and R-20 now own. R-20 reruns R-18's mesh check once
fusion uses confidence.

**Ruled out.** s-05's two frames with ~20 mm R-04 residuals (`IMG_4184`, `IMG_4185`) aren't the
cause: dropping them changed neither the stray-point fraction nor the measured size.

**Things the next person would otherwise rediscover:**
- A matplotlib 3D scatter is not to scale. It stretches each axis separately, and the first renders
  here also had height pointing down (`z` points into the table). They made s-05 look like a cube.
  Set an equal aspect and plot `-z` as height before judging any shape.
- `requirements-gpu.txt` assumes the Lightning Studio's preinstalled PyTorch. On a Mac,
  `pip install torch torchvision` comes first; VGGT fails without `torchvision`.
- The first download of `facebook/VGGT-1B` failed after 80 minutes with a Hugging Face Xet CDN
  error. `HF_HUB_DISABLE_XET=1` switched to plain HTTP and worked.
- The local `fixtures/data/golden_capture/s-03` was a stale 22-frame copy with a different content
  hash. It was replaced, and all five samples were re-verified against
  `golden_capture_manifest.json` before any run.
