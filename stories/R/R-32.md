## Why this exists

R-22 found two reasons parts reconstruct off their CAD size, and R-23 fixed the first, the
focal-length scale error. The second has no story. Cross-sections shrink unevenly from edge to edge,
and R-22 traced that to R-01's per-pixel depth.

It's still there:
- **After R-23,** s-04's edges measured 0.840 (26 mm), 0.936 (65 mm) and 1.002 (60 mm) of CAD.
  s-05's 30 mm edges measured 0.854–0.919.
- **On 2026-11-07 (P-29),** measured per edge with `cad_size_check`, s-04 from all 30 photos gave
  0.886 on the 65 mm edge, 11% short. Across the three photo sets, the edges ran 0.886–1.047.
- **A second measure gives different numbers.** Slicing the observed shell by height and comparing
  its footprint with the CAD sliced the same way, A-12's s-04 input came out 3–9% narrow at
  mid-height. The two measures aren't interchangeable, so the worst case is the 11% edge, not 9%.
- **The shape is off as well as the size,** in surface labelled Observed. On s-04's isolated
  observed shell (A-12's input, 2026-11-04), the long 60 mm wall leans about 3 mm: its position
  moves from −55.4 to −52.2 mm between 10 and 25 mm up. Its lower half is about 5 mm thick, by the
  10–90% spread of its points. The short 26 mm wall stands straight, at 101.6–101.9 mm with a 1–2
  mm spread.

Observed surface is the only kind that can pass or fail a part, so a leaning or thick wall could
produce a false Fail once inspection compares it with CAD. It's the only known error in the part's
own shape that no story owns. R-22 named the next step:
rerun s-04 and s-05 through MASt3R or COLMAP and compare them with `recon/cad_size_check.py`.

## Acceptance criteria

- **Same frames, two or more backends, one measure.** Reconstruct s-04 and s-05, the two golden
  objects with CAD, from the same frames. Use VGGT and at least one other backend: MASt3R, plus
  COLMAP if it completes on these frames. Run each on `main`'s current pipeline with R-23's board
  anchor, and measure it with `recon/cad_size_check.py --cad`. Record side by side, for each run:
  - the board scale and height ratio;
  - every edge's ratio, and its ratio over board scale;
  - the fit residual;
  - **for each flat CAD wall, the surface's offset from that wall by height (its lean) and its
    spread (its thickness).** Measure this twice: on R-01's raw cloud, which is what
    `cad_size_check` reads, and on the fused model's Observed surface. A lean that appears only
    after fusion then reads differently from one already in the depth.
- **Photo-subset noise is measured, not assumed.** Run every backend on all 30 frames and on the
  two 15-frame halves (every other frame, starting from the first and from the second). On
  2026-11-07, the choice of subset alone moved s-04's 65 mm edge from 0.886 to 0.963. A difference
  between backends counts only if it's larger than that spread.
- **A conclusion, with the numbers behind it.** Say which of three it is:
  - VGGT-specific: another backend's edges and walls sit nearer CAD by more than the subset
    spread;
  - shared by every backend, which points at later stages or the capture itself;
  - unresolved.

  Give the answer for size (edge ratios) and for shape (lean and thickness) separately. They may
  not share a cause: a lean that appears only in the fused surface points at fusion, and so at
  R-30's misaligned frames, not at depth.

  Then name the next step. A fix that's more than small becomes its own story, R-22's rule.
- **No accuracy or tolerance claim (D-003).** Every ratio is against nominal CAD, not calipers;
  caliper truth is G-03's and G-04's. FDM drift applies, so the notes say so beside the figures.

## Implementation notes

- **2026-11-09, @mbj1994:** extended recon/cad_size_check.py with the R-32 wall-shape
  measurement needed by the acceptance criteria. Each flat CAD wall now reports signed offset by
  height, a fitted lean (mm and degrees), and p10-p90 thickness. The raw R-01 cloud is measured
  automatically whenever --cad is used.
- Added optional --observed-glb PATH so the same wall-shape measurement is repeated on only
  vertices participating in fully Observed provenance faces. It reuses the raw cloud's CAD wall
  pose instead of fitting a second pose to the fused mesh, so raw/fused offsets stay directly
  comparable and a lean introduced by fusion cannot be absorbed by re-alignment.
- Added a synthetic tilted/thickened wall test that recovers the known 4 degree lean and 2 mm
  full wall band (1.6 mm p10-p90 thickness for the test distribution), plus flat-wall and
  insufficient-evidence cases.
- **Real backend matrix still required on CUDA before close-out:** for both s-04 and s-05, run
  VGGT and MASt3R on all 30 frames plus the two alternating 15-frame halves; run COLMAP on the
  same sets when it completes. Use each backend's default confidence, record failures rather than
  dropping them, and run cad_size_check.py --cad ... --observed-glb <session>/artifacts/uncertainty/mesh_uncertainty.glb for the raw and fused
  wall profiles. No numerical conclusion is recorded here until those real runs exist.

- **2026-11-10, @TabeenRaoof (review fixes on #280, at @mbj1994's request):**
  - **A real sign bug, found while adding the `--observed-glb` test.** `_fit_rigid_2d` tries both
    handednesses, because a blob's handedness relative to the CAD isn't known, so a mirrored fit
    reverses the edges' winding. The synthetic 30 mm box fitted mirrored, reported as
    `rotation_deg` 179.44. Offsets were taken along each edge's left-hand normal, so under a
    mirrored fit every offset and lean flipped sign, run by run. A mirror-symmetric part like
    s-05's square can fit either way. Normals now point towards the fitted footprint's centre,
    whatever the winding.
  - **The sign convention, stated in the code and in every report** (`wall_offset_convention`):
    a positive `offset_mm` is inside the CAD wall, where the part reads small; a positive lean is a
    wall moving inward as it rises.
  - **Matching walls across runs.** For a symmetric footprint, `edge_index` doesn't name the same
    physical wall in two runs, since s-05's square can fit with any edge on any wall. Each profile
    now also carries `midpoint_xy_mm` and `inward_normal_xy` in the board frame. Compare walls
    across backends and subsets by those, not by index.
  - **The fused surface for the acceptance runs is the served `uncertainty/mesh_uncertainty.glb`**,
    with R-10's labels after the sigma downgrade, not `provenance/mesh_provenance.glb`. The report
    records which labels it was given (`fused_observed.labels`), so a pre-downgrade run can't pass
    for a served one.
  - **Tests:** the sign, mirrored and not; only fully Observed faces count; the label naming; and
    `build_report` end to end with `--observed-glb`. That last one checks that a fused wall 1 mm
    outside the CAD reads −1.0 with the raw cloud's pose, while decoys on faces that aren't fully
    Observed are ignored. Reverting the inward-normal fix fails two of them.
  - **Synced with `main`.**

**MASt3R's CAD fits aren't consistent.** All three frame sets show the same physical placement,
so the fitted part should face the same way every time.
- VGGT's fits do: s-04 rotations 6.1° / −1.3° / 1.2°, s-05 3.8–4.1° off square, residuals
  0.7–1.9 mm.
- MASt3R's don't: s-04 at 41.0° / 21.5° / −6.4°, s-05 13° / 26° / 4° off square, residuals
  2.5–4.8 mm.
- So most MASt3R edge ratios fit the wrong lines. Only s-04 half1 and s-05 half1 are registered
  like VGGT's.

**Conclusion (R-32's three-way rule):**
- **Size: unresolved.** VGGT's shortfall is repeatable: s-05 about 12% short on every set, with a
  spread of 0.004, and s-04's 65 mm edge 4–11% short. But no second backend measured it like for
  like: MASt3R's fits are inconsistent, and COLMAP refused every set. So "VGGT-specific" can't be
  shown or ruled out.
- **Shape: unresolved for the backend question.** For VGGT, leans are 1.7 mm or less in size and
  about the same raw and fused, so fusion doesn't add lean; it is already in the depth. MASt3R's
  shape numbers sit on the same unreliable fits.

**Next steps.** Per R-22's rule, anything more than small becomes its own story.
