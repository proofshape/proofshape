## Why this exists

#231 named two separate problems in the served model. The first, the board skin, is R-30's. This
story is the second: two-thirds of the served mesh's faces are Unobserved surface reaching from
about 10 mm up to the top of the fusion volume, spanning the whole board area, mostly
near-vertical. The raw fusion mesh shows the same thing: its largest connected component spans
the entire volume. A fresh re-measurement on `main` (review on #258, @TabeenRaoof, 30-frame
s-04 run) found it essentially unchanged: 70.3% of faces are "walls" by the same centroid
classification, labelled 0% Observed.

`extract_mesh_marching_cubes` already masks `weight == 0` voxels, so these walls come from voxels
some frame genuinely updated — the root cause was explicitly left unestablished in #231, and
still is.

**Why this isn't R-30's problem too, as far as the evidence goes:** R-30 corrects where each
frame's *board plane* sits — an offset/tilt applied per frame before fusion, aimed at the board
skin. `fuse_tsdf`'s per-voxel update (`recon/tsdf_fusion.py`) has no step that requires more than
one frame's agreement before trusting a near-zero crossing as a real surface — `weight` just
counts how many frames contributed at all, and marching cubes only checks `weight > 0`, not how
many of those contributions actually agreed the voxel was *on* the surface versus merely in front
of or behind it. With a limited number of viewpoints (30 frames, mostly looking down at a small
part on a large board), a single frame's single noisy depth sample can leave a stray near-zero
crossing at a voxel that isn't really on any surface — a well-known failure mode of fusing from
sparse viewpoints with no visibility/consensus constraint, not a symptom of per-frame board
misalignment. If that diagnosis is right, R-30 landing first will not change these numbers; this
story should measure that directly before designing a fix (see Acceptance criteria).

**Why sequenced after R-30, not folded into it:** any fix here will very likely land in
`recon/tsdf_fusion.py`, the same file R-30 changes, and the two likely have different root
causes (per-frame pose vs. per-voxel vote consensus). Folding them would conflate two
investigations under one story's criteria and inflate R-30's own estimate for work that isn't
re-anchoring. Sequencing — this story branches from `main` only after R-30 merges — avoids the
same-file conflict without the conflation; @TabeenRaoof flagged the choice as the owner's call
(review on #241/R-26's follow-up, #258) and was fine with either.

## Acceptance criteria

- The diagnosis above is checked, not assumed: measure the wall share (the same centroid
  classification #231 and #258 used) on the five golden objects with R-30 already applied, and
  record whether it actually changes. If R-30 does shrink the walls meaningfully, say so plainly
  and revise this story's scope before designing a fix for a problem that turned out smaller than
  measured.
- The root cause is established on real data, not assumed from the mechanism described above:
  at a sample of wall voxels/faces, measure how many frames actually voted them as "on the
  surface" (near-zero signed distance) versus merely "in front of" or "behind" a surface,
  compared to the same measurement at voxels that end up as real, kept part geometry. A
  consensus-based explanation predicts wall voxels are decided by very few frames (often one);
  real part surface should show much broader agreement.
- A fix is implemented and justified by that measurement — a minimum-frame-agreement threshold
  before trusting a near-zero crossing, a visibility/space-carving step, or whatever the
  established cause actually calls for. Record why the chosen fix addresses the measured cause,
  not just that it reduces the wall count.
- The five golden objects are rerun with the fix, and the wall share is measured and recorded for
  each, the same way #231/#258 measured it, so this doesn't repeat R-26's mistake of shipping
  without a golden rerun on the final head.
- `observed_fraction` and anything else that currently isolates "the part" by clustering/height
  heuristics (A-04's shape-completion stage, next-view scoring, the inspection registration lane)
  is checked against the reduced wall surface — not expected to need code changes, but the
  reduction in extraneous Unobserved geometry should be recorded since those consumers currently
  work around exactly this artifact.

## Tests

- a synthetic scene built to have a single-frame spurious near-zero crossing (an isolated voxel
  one frame votes "on surface" that every other frame votes "empty" or doesn't see at all) is
  excluded from the fixed extraction, where it would have survived `weight > 0`-only masking;
- a synthetic scene with genuine broad multi-frame agreement at the true surface is unaffected by
  the fix;
- the five golden objects, with the fix applied, measured end to end (wall face share, frame-vote
  consensus at a wall-vs-part sample) and the figures recorded in this story's notes.

- **Second acceptance criterion, the frame-vote measurement tool:** the consensus mechanism
  suspected in "Why this exists" above (`fuse_tsdf` trusting a near-zero crossing from however
  few frames said anything, with no requirement that several agree) needed a way to measure it
  directly, not assume it. `recon.tsdf_fusion.measure_voxel_vote_consensus` reuses `fuse_tsdf`'s
  own per-frame `contributes`/`signed_distance` logic (same occlusion rule: a frame farther
  behind the surface than `truncation_mm` says nothing about a point, excluded from every count
  here too) but, instead of averaging into one TSDF value per voxel, classifies and keeps every
  contributing frame's own vote -- `near_surface` (within `near_surface_mm`, default one voxel,
  of zero signed distance), `in_front`, or `behind` -- for a small handful of sample points
  rather than the whole dense grid. `fuse_run` gained an optional `vote_sample_points_mm`
  parameter that runs this on its own already-computed depth/extrinsic/keep_mask and writes
  `vote_consensus.json` alongside `tsdf_fusion.json`, the same way R-30's `corrected_extrinsic.npz`
  was added.
  `scripts/measure_frame_vote_consensus.py` ties this to criterion 1's tool: it samples wall and
  part points from an existing `mesh_provenance.glb` via `measure_wall_share`'s own
  classification, then reruns `fuse_run` a second time (wasteful, but keeps the vote logic in
  step with `fuse_tsdf`'s real rules instead of a second, possibly-drifted copy) with those
  points as `vote_sample_points_mm`, reproducing the first run's `voxel_size_mm`,
  `truncation_mm`, `height_threshold_mm` and `min_confidence` exactly from its own
  `tsdf_fusion.json`. Reports the median vote counts, wall group vs. part group.
  **Not covered:** `min_kept_points`, `max_part_height_mm`, `max_board_tilt_deg` and
  `footprint_mm` aren't recorded in `tsdf_fusion.json` and aren't overridable by this script
  either -- it assumes the first run left them at their defaults, true of every `fuse_run` call
  in this codebase so far.

- **Criterion 2's real answer, measured by @TabeenRaoof on the five golden objects
  (`main` at `ce18958`, review comment on #231), 50 wall and 50 part samples per object, seed 0,
  every rerun checked against reproducing the original fusion's face/vertex counts:**

  | Object | Walls with zero contributing frames | Walls with <=1 near-surface vote | Part: median contributing | Part: median near-surface | Part with <=1 near-surface vote |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | s-01 | 98% | 100% | 14 | 7 | 6% |

  | s-03 | 94% | 100% | 14 | 6.5 | 18% |
  | s-04 | 94% | 100% | 17.5 | 6 | 0% |
  | s-05 | 90% | 100% | 11.5 | 6 | 6% |

  **The story's own hypothesis was half right, and the real mechanism is more specific than "too
  few frames agree."** At 90-98% of wall points, no frame says *anything at all* -- not "one frame
  voted it near-surface", but zero votes of any kind -- while real part surface gets broad
  multi-frame agreement (median 11.5-17.5 contributing frames). So this isn't a sparse-viewpoint
  consensus failure in the sense R-33's "Why this exists" describes (a single noisy frame's
  near-zero crossing nothing else corroborates); it's something building surface where *no frame
  ever measured anything*.

  **The likely mechanism, demonstrated synthetically by @TabeenRaoof, not yet checked voxel by
  voxel against the real golden grids:** `fuse_tsdf` initialises every voxel's TSDF to `0.0`,
  which is exactly `extract_mesh_marching_cubes`'s own `level=0.0`. That function's docstring
  claims `mask=weight>0` keeps unobserved space from ever fabricating a surface -- scikit-image
  0.24 does not actually guarantee this at a mask boundary. On a synthetic grid with no real
  surface at all, just an observed free-space block (TSDF +8) next to never-observed voxels
  (TSDF 0.0, masked out), `marching_cubes` still built 541 faces along the boundary between them:
  the never-observed voxels' untouched `0.0` sits exactly at `level=0.0`, so the boundary itself
  reads as a zero-crossing regardless of the mask. This fits every measurement so far: 0% Observed,
  about 80% near-vertical, reaching 147-149 mm in the 150 mm volume, and unchanged by R-30 (R-30
  corrects per-frame board pose, not this).

  **Two notes for criterion 3 (the fix), from @TabeenRaoof's own synthetic probing:**
  - Initialising unobserved voxels to `+truncation_mm` instead of `0.0` removes the walls next to
    observed free space, but not next to observed *behind-surface* voxels -- a smaller wall still
    forms there (550 to 351 faces on the same synthetic test). Skipping any cube with an
    unobserved corner entirely, rather than relying on skimage's own `mask` parameter, looks
    cleaner than either initial value.
  - A volume with no real zero-crossing anywhere (every voxel at `+truncation_mm`) would newly
    crash: scikit-image raises `ValueError: Surface level must be within volume data range`, and
    `extract_mesh_marching_cubes` only catches `RuntimeError`'s "No surface found" message, not
    this. Any fix touching the initial value has to handle this case too.

  Per-sample results (`wall_vs_part_votes.json`, `vote_consensus.json`, `tsdf_fusion.json`, run
  logs, all five objects) are on @TabeenRaoof's machine -- points and vote counts only, no
  photos; ask if they'd help design the fix.

- **Third acceptance criterion, the fix.** An Explore agent independently reproduced the
  mechanism against the actual installed scikit-image (0.24.0) and confirmed the installed
  behaviour directly (no `.pyx` source ships in this venv's compiled extension to read instead).
  A Plan agent then brute-forced the exact activation rule skimage uses: **a single `mask=True`
  corner out of a cube's 8 is enough** to trigger interpolation using the other 7 corners' raw
  stored values, regardless of their own mask status -- worse than "somewhere between 1 and 4",
  which makes defending against *any* threshold the right call rather than tuning toward a
  guessed one.

  **The fix:** `extract_mesh_marching_cubes` now erodes `observed_mask` by a full 3x3x3
  neighbourhood (`scipy.ndimage.binary_erosion`, `border_value=True` so the volume's own outer
  edge isn't eroded just for being an edge) before passing it to skimage as `mask`, instead of
  passing `observed_mask` directly. A voxel survives erosion only if *every* cube it could be a
  corner of (up to 8) is itself fully observed -- so a cube with one bad corner has all 8 of its
  corners erased, not just that one, which structurally prevents it from ever presenting a
  `True` corner to skimage regardless of the real activation threshold. `fuse_tsdf`'s own TSDF
  initialisation (`0.0`) is unchanged; only the mask `extract_mesh_marching_cubes` builds from it
  is different. A second, pre-existing gap in the same function is also closed: skimage's level-
  range check runs over the *entire raw volume*, ignoring `mask`, and raises `ValueError` when
  nothing in the array crosses zero at all -- reachable even without this fix (just rare, since a
  real grid almost always has an untouched `0.0` voxel somewhere), and now caught the same way
  the existing `RuntimeError`("No surface found") case already was.

  **Correcting one assumption from criterion 2's own notes, found while testing:** the "behind-
  surface" boundary case recorded above (from the *rejected* `+truncation_mm`-initialisation
  idea) doesn't apply to this fix, and isn't actually a bug under the *current* `0.0`
  initialisation either -- checked directly: an isolated observed-occupied (negative tsdf) voxel
  next to never-observed space does not reproduce any spurious face, with or without this fix.
  The real bug is asymmetric: only an observed **free-space** (positive tsdf) region near
  never-observed space triggers it, and only when that region is thin/isolated enough in at
  least two axes that its own cubes have a minority of real corners -- a full-width flat slab,
  checked directly too, does not trigger it either. This matches the real golden-data symptom
  (walls read 0% Observed, i.e. empty/unobserved-labelled, not occupied) and is consistent with
  ragged, multi-view camera coverage rather than a flat gap.

  **Side effect, measured not assumed:** the fix erodes the extracted surface by one voxel at
  every true observed/unobserved boundary. Whether that visibly shrinks real part geometry on
  the golden set is for the golden rerun below to show, not to guess at here.

  Tests (`tests/test_tsdf_fusion.py`, all synthetic): the exact bug reproduced and fixed (a thin
  free-space sliver next to never-observed space -- confirmed the *old*, un-eroded mask really
  does fabricate a face here, so the test isn't vacuous); the new `ValueError` path; erosion
  removing every voxel (a single interior voxel with no full neighbourhood of its own); the two
  pre-existing `extract_mesh_marching_cubes` tests confirmed unaffected by hand (a fully-observed
  grid and a fully-unobserved one are both unchanged by erosion with `border_value=True`).
