## Why this exists

D-060 (from the review on #241/R-26) traces the "raised board layer" R-17 found 4-6 mm above the
real board on two of the five golden objects to its actual cause: it is not a second physical
layer. It is the real board, as seen by a handful of frames whose VGGT pose is misaligned by a
few millimetres. R-23's alignment is one global similarity transform fit once across every
frame, so it cannot correct this per-frame drift.

Three rounds of R-26 tried to fix this downstream, in `measure_board_surface`'s layer-top search,
each catching the previous round's synthetic failure while introducing a new real-golden-data
one (round 3 cuts too low on s-02 through s-05 and fails s-01 outright). The reason every attempt
failed the same way: on the *merged* point cloud, this misalignment artifact and a genuine flat
part's own top are the same shape -- a dense planar patch a few millimetres above the board --
and no classifier built from height/location data on the merged cloud alone can tell them apart.
R-26 closes without solving this (D-060); it stays on `main`'s original search plus the explicit
`height_threshold_mm` override.

This story fixes it where the information actually still exists: per-frame, before the clouds are
merged. `fuse_run` already has each frame's own points and extrinsic. Re-anchoring each frame to
its own board plane, rather than trusting its pose as given, removes the misalignment at its
source instead of trying to classify around it afterward.

It also shrinks #231's board-skin artifact: one frame in that fresh s-04 run sits 3.0 mm above
the board with the cut at 2.5 mm, straddling the cut and surviving into fusion as skin on the
part. The same per-frame correction addresses that case too.

## Acceptance criteria

- Each frame's own board-plane offset is measured (within `fuse_run`'s existing board-candidate
  points for that frame) and corrected before that frame's points enter the merged cloud, so a
  misaligned frame's board no longer reads as an elevated layer.
- A frame with too little visible board to fit a plane raises/refuses explicitly rather than
  silently skipping correction or guessing -- the residual risk @TabeenRaoof's prototype flagged
  (every golden frame had enough board; a close-up phone frame might not).
- With re-anchoring applied, the five golden objects' height cut and kept geometry are measured
  and recorded, and compared explicitly against `main`'s existing (R-26-closed) cuts and against
  each other -- this changes every object's cut (D-060's prototype moved them from 3-6.5 mm down
  to about 2 mm), so this is not expected to match `main`'s numbers, only to be measured and
  recorded rather than assumed.
- `measure_board_surface`'s "first density gap from zero" search (the one round 1 of R-26
  rejected before the ghost layer was understood) is re-evaluated against the re-anchored cloud:
  D-060's prototype found it becomes correct once the ghost layer is gone. Record whether that
  holds, rather than reintroducing R-26's per-cell/connectivity machinery.

## Tests

- a synthetic frame whose own board plane is offset from the others is corrected into agreement,
  and a thin flat part placed on that frame's own board is no longer read as a second layer;
- a frame with too few board points to fit a plane raises rather than silently passing through
  uncorrected;
- the five golden objects, re-anchored, measured end to end (board σ, height cut, kept part
  geometry) and the figures recorded in this story's notes, not just asserted to "look better".

## Notes and decisions

- Follows from D-060 (R-26/#241's close-out). R-26 itself stays small and does not depend on
  this story landing.
- @TabeenRaoof's prototype (posted on #241) was a crude per-point vertical shift, not a proper
  extrinsic correction, and found per-frame tilt residuals up to 2.3 degrees -- the real fix
  should correct the frame's extrinsic (3 degrees of freedom), not just shift z. Estimate is
  @TabeenRaoof's own figure (4-5 h) plus the golden rerun, explicitly given as an estimate, not
  measured.
- This changes every golden object's fused output. R-24 and R-25's recorded figures were measured
  against the pre-re-anchoring geometry and must be re-measured against the corrected one, not
  assumed to still hold.
- Also addresses #231's board-skin artifact (a frame straddling the segmentation cut survives
  into fusion as skin on the part) -- the same per-frame drift, a different symptom.
