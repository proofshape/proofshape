## Why this exists

The capture page has no stylesheet. C-07's guidance arrow is a text glyph (`&#8593;`) at body
font size. It sits in `#shell-gyro-overlay`, below the video and the status lines
(`capture/src/shell.ts`, `APP_SHELL_HTML`), so on an iPhone it is tiny and often scrolled off the
bottom of the screen. "Capture frame" is a default-sized button. Nothing on screen says whether
the phone is at the next target until a frame has been taken. The sprint plan's S6 capture work
includes "UI bugs"; this is one, found by @TabeenRaoof while preparing a class demo.

## Acceptance criteria

- **The arrow is visible and in view.** It is drawn over the camera preview, at least 72 px
  across, in one calm colour (not black, not animated beyond its rotation). Its direction comes
  from `computeArrowGuidance`, unchanged. The preview is never cropped, so the photo is what the
  preview shows.
- **The preview's frame says where the phone is:**
  - **green** when the phone is aligned with the next target: the live reading is within the 15°
    coverage tolerance, and coverage's own nearest-slot rule would assign a photo taken there to
    that target. Green describes the live alignment only. A photo counts toward coverage once it
    is captured and the server accepts it, using that frame's own gyro reading;
  - **amber** within twice that tolerance;
  - **red** beyond it;
  - neutral before the first frame (there is no target yet);
  - green once the orbit is complete.
- **The shutter is a large round button** (at least 72 px) at the bottom centre of the preview.
  It keeps its id, its accessible name "Capture frame", its disabled rules, and a visible
  keyboard focus ring.

## Tests

In `capture/src/`, written with the code:
- `orbit.test.ts`: the level function at its 15° and 30° boundaries, and across the ±180°
  azimuth wrap.
- `overlay.test.ts`: the reported level follows dispatched orientation events, through
  `complete`.
- `shell.test.ts`: the viewfinder's `data-guidance` follows the overlay. The shutter keeps its id
  and accessible name.
- `pages-build.test.ts`: the built page carries the stylesheet.

A unit test can't see size, colour or scrolling on a phone. That part is a written device check on
the iPhone, recorded in the notes.
