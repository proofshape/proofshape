## Why this exists

`_board_pose_from_yaw` (`inspect/inspection/registration.py`) seeds every candidate pose by
moving the CAD's centre onto the mean of all Observed model points. That is the part's centre
only if the model is only the part. P-41's review (`docs/accuracy-review-2026-11-20.md`,
`main` at `607450a`) found it often isn't:

- **Stray surface is a large share of some models.** On six of the ten calipered parts, 12 to
  44% of the Observed vertices are more than 5 mm from the part. They sit a median 3 to 4 mm
  above the board, just over the cut. This is #292's kind of board skin, on more parts than #292
  names. I-14 keeps it out of the verdict, because it is outside the CAD's outline. Nothing
  keeps it out of the pose search.
- **The alignment is weak where that share is high.** Fitness is 0.37 to 0.77 on five of the
  ten parts. On `main`'s own meshes, the poses of s-05 and the hexagon are about 10° and 16°
  away from the pose found once the photos are re-anchored (R-44), beyond what their symmetry
  allows.
- **It can turn a good part's pose with no flag.** With R-44's correction on and nothing else
  changed, the G-05 control comes out 180° off, not flagged ambiguous, and reads 9.80 mm
  against 5.58 mm. Started from `main`'s pose, the same mesh reads 5.22 mm, so the mesh is fine
  and the search is not. In six other scratch variants the control came out 90° off, unflagged.
- **Two saved phone sessions fail outright** on `main` with "No candidate could be refined
  (… 0 Observed-model/CAD correspondences within 4.5 mm …)". Their photographs were not
  opened, so what was on the board is not verified. They are recorded as cases to look at, not
  as proof.

A quick trial in the same scratch harness fitted the alignment on the upper half of the model
only (above half of its own Observed height). On `main`'s meshes the median fitness went from
0.81 to 0.99 (lowest 0.37 → 0.57). With R-44's rigid correction on, it was 1.00 (lowest 0.64,
`g02_01`), eight of ten parts Passed at 3.7 mm, and every pose was within 3° of the one found by
starting the search from `main`'s pose on the same mesh. It is one rule at one setting, so it is
evidence that the cause is right, not a chosen fix: on one other variant it landed 19° off, and
flagged itself ambiguous.

R-44's third criterion needs this: its correction can't go on in the served path while the
control's pose turns.

## Acceptance criteria

- **The pose search no longer follows stray surface.** The seeds, and whatever the fit matches
  against, come from the part by a stated rule. The story records the rule, why it was chosen,
  and what else was tried (the upper-half trial above is one candidate, a robust centre is
  another). A synthetic model with stray Observed surface planted a few millimetres above the
  board, away from the part, registers to the same pose as the same model without it.
- **A pose that can't be trusted is flagged or refused, never served as a clean one.** The
  cases above are re-run and recorded: the G-05 control with R-44's correction on, and the two
  phone sessions. Each either lands on the right pose or comes back ambiguous or failed.
- **Nothing that works today gets worse, on record.** The ten calipered parts are run before
  and after, on `main`'s meshes and with R-44's correction on: pose against the reference,
  fitness, and the ambiguity flag. D-061's synthetic table is re-run and its classes still flag
  as they do now. D-061's cutoff and the contract are untouched.

## Tests

Unit tests are written with the change, on I-01's synthetic samples: a part with planted stray
surface against the same part without it; a notched plate (the G-05 shape) with stray surface,
which must not come back turned and unflagged; and D-061's classes unchanged. The real-capture
runs are a written check, with commands and numbers in this file. Captures stay outside git.
