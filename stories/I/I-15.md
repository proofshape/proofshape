## Why this exists

P-41's review (`docs/accuracy-review-2026-11-20.md`, `main` at `607450a`) found that a served
model often holds surface that isn't the part, and that the CAD pose can turn because of it.

**What the code does** (`inspect/inspection/registration.py`). `global_register` works on a
sample of the whole model surface, Observed or not (`_sample_surface(model_vertices,
model_faces, …)`). It has two seed paths:

- **Yaw seeds.** `_board_pose_from_yaw` turns the CAD to each seeded yaw and moves its centroid
  onto the centroid of that model sample.
- **Projected RANSAC.** `_project_ransac_to_board_pose` takes the yaw from an FPFH and RANSAC
  fit, and keeps RANSAC's own xy translation.

Both are scored against the same whole-surface sample, at a distance that scales with the
extent of the model and the CAD together (`_extent_mm`). Only after that do `refine` and
`check_ambiguity` restrict themselves to Observed support.

So surface that isn't the part can reach the result at several stages. It moves the centroid the
yaw seeds start from. It stretches the extent that sets the search radii. It is part of what
every candidate is scored against. And where it is labelled Observed, it is part of what
refinement fits to.

**What was measured:**

- **Stray surface is a large share of some models.** On six of the ten calipered parts, 12 to
  44% of the Observed vertices are more than 5 mm from the part. They sit a median 3 to 4 mm
  above the board, just over the cut. This is #292's kind of board skin, on more parts than #292
  names. I-14 keeps it out of the verdict, because it is outside the CAD's outline. Nothing
  keeps it out of the pose search.
- **The alignment is weak where that share is high.** Fitness is 0.37 to 0.77 on five of the
  ten parts. On `main`'s own meshes, the poses of s-05 and the hexagon are about 10° and 16°
  away from the pose found once the photos are re-anchored (R-44), beyond what their symmetry
  allows. Which of the two is right isn't known: there is no independent pose for either yet.
- **It can turn a good part's pose with no flag.** With R-44's correction on and nothing else
  changed, the G-05 control comes out 180° off, not flagged ambiguous, and reads 9.80 mm
  against 5.58 mm. Started from `main`'s pose, the same mesh reads 5.22 mm. In six other scratch
  variants the control came out 90° off, unflagged.
- **Two saved phone sessions fail outright** on `main` with "No candidate could be refined
  (… 0 Observed-model/CAD correspondences within 4.5 mm …)". Their photographs were not
  opened, so what was on the board is not verified.

**What isn't known: which stage loses the right pose.** A scratch trial marked Observed surface
below half the model's height as Unobserved before registration. That changes only what `refine`
and `check_ambiguity` see, because the global search samples every face whatever its label. It
still lifted the median fitness on `main`'s meshes from 0.81 to 0.99 (lowest 0.37 → 0.57), and
with R-44's rigid correction on, eight of ten parts Passed at 3.7 mm. So part of the problem is
in the Observed-support stages. The trial says nothing about the seeds. On one other variant it
landed the control 19° off, and flagged it.

R-44's third criterion needs this story: its correction can't go on in the served path while the
control's pose turns.

## How a pose is judged, here and in R-44

There is no ground-truth pose for any capture today, and several of `main`'s own poses are in
doubt. So "right" and "same" are defined before anything is measured:

- **Two references, neither from the pipeline's pose.** The photographs give what a coarse
  reading can: for each capture used, its owner reads the part's placement against the board's
  printed grid, namely which face is down, which way a named CAD edge points, to the nearest
  10°, and where the part's centre sits, to the nearest 10 mm. The reading and the photo it came
  from go into this story before any comparison is run. G-06 and G-08 already record which way
  the numbered sides faced for some parts, and those are used where they exist. For fine yaw,
  the reference is the model's own walls: the direction of each straight wall in horizontal
  slices of the Observed model, against the matching edge of the posed CAD. That involves no
  fitting of the CAD to the model, so it can't inherit the search's error. The record names the
  parts whose CAD has straight walls.
- **A pose is right** if all of these hold:
  - it rests on the face the photographs show, where the CAD's faces differ. Flipping s-05 or a
    box-shaped part onto its opposite face gives the same surface, and a photo can't tell those
    faces apart, so they aren't distinguished;
  - its yaw is within 20° of the photo reading, which catches a pose turned 90° or 180°;
  - for a part with straight walls, its yaw is also within 5° of the walls;
  - its centre is within 15 mm of the reading, or within half the CAD's smallest footprint
    dimension if that is smaller. `g02_01` is a 20 mm square, so its margin is 10 mm. A flat 15 mm
    would let a pose that slid onto stray surface beside a small part still count as right.

  The 20° and 15 mm margins are the reading's own precision with a margin. The 5° wall margin is
  there because 20° can't see the error this story was written for: `main`'s s-05 and hexagon
  poses are 9.3 to 11.9° and 15.2 to 15.7° off their walls, inside 20°, while the re-anchored
  poses are within 1.3° and 0.3° (#426).
- **A pose the search can't reach is a known limit, recorded, not judged.** `registration.py`
  searches yaw about the board normal plus x and y. It can't turn the CAD over. For golden s-04,
  the outline check posted on #427 found the registered CAD to be the mirror image of the model,
  most likely because the block lay on its other triangular face. It comes back unflagged on all
  three scratch variants (coverage ratio 1.048, 1.505 and 1.914 against D-061's 1.025 cutoff,
  #440). Making it flag would need a handedness check, a new capability. So if the photo reading
  confirms the mirror, golden s-04 is recorded here as a known limit, counted neither as a pass
  nor a failure of any criterion, and the check goes to a follow-up story, I-16, after the
  freeze.
  - **The reading confirms it** (2026-11-24, table below): in `IMG_4093` s-04 shows the other
    hand from its CAD, so it lay on its other triangular face. Golden s-04 is a known limit
    here. I-16's story is written in its own pull request.
- **Symmetric CADs are compared modulo their symmetry.** About the board normal that is 90° for
  s-05 and `g02_01`, 180° for `g02_02` and `g02_06`, 72° for `g02_04` and 60° for `g02_05`. s-04,
  `g02_03`, `g05_00` and `g05_02` have none. This is for the comparison only. In the service a
  symmetric part still flags ambiguous and comes back Rescan (D-061), and this story doesn't
  change that.
- **Two poses are the same** if the two posed CAD surfaces lie within 2 mm of each other
  everywhere, which is one fusion voxel. A pose turned by a symmetry of the CAD gives the same
  surface, so this needs no list of symmetries.
  - **What 2 mm separates, from the scratch run.** @TabeenRaoof compared `main`'s pose with the
    re-anchored (R-44) pose on the ten parts, in a scratch run reported on #439. Four count as
    the same: `g02_02` 0.27, `g05_02` 0.90, `g02_06` 1.17 and s-04 1.29 mm. Six don't:

    | Part | Surface apart | Yaw apart | Centre apart |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | `g02_04` | 2.09 mm | 0.5° | 2.1 mm |
    | `g02_03` | 2.38 mm | 0.3° | 2.1 mm |
    | s-05 | 2.82 mm | 10.6° | 0.2 mm |
    | `g02_05` | 3.64 mm | 16.1° | 0.7 mm |
    | `g02_01` | about 10.1 mm | 4.4° | 9.5 mm |
    | `g05_00` | about 10.1 mm | 178.2° | 9.4 mm |

    Yaw is taken modulo the CAD's symmetry. Yaw and centre were measured by @TabeenRaoof on
    #440 from each variant's `measure.npz`. Five of the six are inside the 20° and 15 mm photo
    margins. s-05 and `g02_05` are the cases the wall margin above exists for.
  - **"Same" is recorded, not blocking.** Here, criterion 4 blocks only on a part that was right
    and isn't after; "same" is reported beside it. This story proposes the same for R-44's gate
    (see R-44's third criterion): blocking there on "same" would hold back R-44's default on
    parts where its correction moved one right pose to another. With the wall margin in "right",
    a pose that only turned 10 to 16° no longer counts as right, so not blocking on "same" no
    longer lets that error through. The definition and its 2 mm stay as they are.
- **An unverified capture counts for nothing.** A capture whose contents its owner hasn't
  confirmed from the photographs is listed as unverified. It is neither evidence that the
  search works nor evidence that it fails. `sess_cdf9822969a4` is in that state (see the
  readings below).

The margins above (20°, 15 mm or half the smallest footprint dimension, 5° from the walls, and
2 mm) are this story's proposals. The claim's reviewers agree them or change them now, so they
aren't fitted to the result.

The scratch numbers were already known when this section was re-stated on 2026-11-23 and
amended on #440's review the same day. They prompted three changes: what blocks, the wall
margin, and the scaled centre margin. None of the 20°, 15 mm and 2 mm margins moved. No
real-capture comparison for this story has been run. That is why the changes are written down
here, with their reasons, for @TabeenRaoof and @mbj1994 to agree or reject before the readings
below are compared with anything.

## Acceptance criteria

- **The stage that loses the right pose is named first, and fixed there.** For the G-05 control
  with R-44's correction on, the record shows whether a candidate near the reference leaves the
  global search from either seed path, whether it survives candidate selection, and what
  refinement does with it. The fix goes where the record points, by a stated rule, with what
  else was tried. A synthetic model with stray surface planted a few millimetres above the
  board, away from the part, then registers to the same pose as the same model without it.
- **Refinement and the ambiguity check keep their safeguards.** `refine` and `check_ambiguity`
  still use Observed support only. D-061's rule, its cutoff and its reason code are unchanged,
  its synthetic table is re-run, and its classes still flag as they do now. Nothing in the
  contract changes.
- **A pose that isn't right is flagged or refused, never served as a clean one.** The cases
  above are re-run and recorded against the reference: the G-05 control with R-44's correction
  on, s-05 and the hexagon on `main`'s meshes, and any phone session whose contents are
  confirmed and which is a pose-search case (see the readings for why neither failing session
  is, today). Each lands on a right pose, or comes back ambiguous or failed.
- **Nothing that works today gets worse, on record.** The ten calipered parts are run before and
  after, on `main`'s meshes and with R-44's correction on. For each: whether the pose is right,
  whether it is the same as before, fitness, and the ambiguity flag. A part whose pose was right
  before and isn't after blocks the change.

### Criterion 1: the stage that loses the right pose, on the G-05 control with R-44's correction on

Run on 2026-11-24 against `g05_00`'s reading above, on @TabeenRaoof's scratch meshes
(`runs/i15-scratch-meshes-2026-11-23/`, all 682 files checked against `SHA256SUMS`), made on
`607450a`. `main` has had no commits under `inspect/` or `recon/` since. The trace outputs are
outside git, in `~/proofshape-runs/i15-traces/` on @dalwalyk's machine.

```
PYTHONPATH=inspect OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 .venv/bin/python -I scripts/trace_pose_search.py \
  --model <bundle>/g05_00/variants/<R or base>/uncertainty/mesh_uncertainty.glb \
  --cad fixtures/reference_cad/g05_00_control_50x32x20mm.stl \
  --faces far --turned 0 --clockwise --squares-from-left 4.0 --squares-from-caption 2.5 \
  --face matches --side-normal 0 1 --symmetry 360 --straight-walls --out <new file>.json
```

**Before the fix, on `R` (R-44's correction on):**

| Stage | A right candidate there? | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| Seeds | yes, 2 of 75 | `yaw:5` is the top seed by fitness (0.871); `yaw:0` third. RANSAC's one candidate isn't right. |
| Deduplication | yes | both survive |
| Selection | yes | `yaw:5` is selected (9 kept) |
| Refinement | yes | `yaw:5` refines to 2.9° from the reading and 2.4° from the walls, fitness 0.594 |

Outcome: **served wrong, unflagged.** The coverage ratio preferred the turned pose too (the
right pose's residual was 1.077 × the turned one's, outside D-061's 1.025), so nothing flagged
it. 37% of the Observed vertices were more than 5 mm from the served pose.

**Why the turned pose won.** Placed at `main`'s saved pose for this mesh, which is right by the
reading, 25% of `R`'s Observed vertices are more than 5 mm from the CAD. Those stray points sit
a median 4 mm above the board (10th to 90th percentile 3.1 to 6.9 mm), and 48 of them lie inside
the plate's notch. Turned 180°, the CAD puts its solid corner over the notch. On a model where
the right pose and the turned pose are within 2% of each other in fitness, that is enough.

**The rule the fix follows.** The right pose survived every stage of the search and was lost
only at `check_ambiguity`'s final ranking, so the fix goes there, and into `refine`, which
produces the poses ranked. Both now leave out surface lower than
`min(5 mm, ¼ of the CAD's height)` above the board: the Observed model faces and, in the
coverage ratio, the CAD's own samples alike. The seeds, `global_register`, the deviation and the
verdict are unchanged. D-061's rule, its 1.025 cutoff and its reason code are unchanged.
@dalwalyk chose the rule on 2026-11-24, before any of the other nine parts was compared.

**What else was tried** (scratch, on the same two meshes and the registration tests):

- **A cut on the model side only, 3, 5, 8 or 10 mm.** Each one made `R` come out right and kept
  `base` right. But a flat 5 mm cut broke three rows of D-061's synthetic table (its 8 mm
  shifted-hole plate came back ambiguous) and the mirror-pick test, because it removed most of
  the plate's walls.
- **`min(5 mm, ¼ height)`, model side only.** Eleven table rows broke. The coverage ratio still
  scored the CAD's own samples below the cut, which now had nothing near them in any pose. The
  identical penalty every pose took squeezed the ratio toward 1. Cutting the CAD's samples the
  same way fixed it: all 151 registration tests pass.
- **@TabeenRaoof's `R+t`, Observed surface below half the model's height relabelled.** It lands
  `R` right, but it discards half of every part's surface and wasn't tried against D-061's table.
- **Synthetic reproductions.** A flat sheet of board skin, up to 86% of the Observed surface,
  and filler in a degraded plate's notch both failed to turn a synthetic G-05 plate, with or
  without the cut. A clean synthetic plate keeps too much margin. The cut did widen the right
  pose's margin on all six seeds tried (for example 1.210 to 1.353), and that is what the
  notched-plate test pins.
