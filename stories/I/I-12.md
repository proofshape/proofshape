## Why this exists

I-01's tests still pass with any one of these mutations applied (#199):

- the noise scaled down 100-fold;
- the seed ignored;
- the base moved off z = 0;
- the random yaw removed;
- noise applied in every direction instead of along the normals;
- a wrong σ written to the sidecar.

I-03…I-05 test registration on this generator's output, so weak tests here could let registration
pass for the wrong reason on near-noiseless, unrotated input. The generator also assumes the
CAD's minimum z is 0 without checking it, and I-02 loads coordinates as stored.

## Acceptance criteria

- On a dense seeded mesh, the noise along the normals has a standard deviation close to σ, and
  the noise across them is close to zero. The tests state their tolerances and why.
- Different seeds give different poses, the output spans z from about −h to 0, and the sidecar's
  `sigma_mm` equals the argument.
- The generator refuses or records a CAD whose minimum z isn't 0.

## Tests

The criteria are the tests. Check each new test against the mutation it targets, as listed in
#199: with that mutation applied, the test must fail. Record that it did.

2026-10-30: Implementation. Six new tests in `tests/test_synthetic.py`, each mapped to one of
#199's six mutations. Two of them needed two tests apiece to close the real gap, not just the
symptom:

- **Noise magnitude and direction** (σ scaled 100x; isotropic instead of along-normal): one test
  decomposes `apply_gaussian_noise`'s output into along-normal and tangential components on a
  densified mesh (many vertices, stable statistics). Along-normal std is checked against σ with a
  25% relative tolerance (loose enough not to be flaky, tight enough to fail a 100x-off σ hard);
  tangential is checked near machine precision, since displacement is `offset * normal` by
  construction in the correct implementation.
- **Seed and yaw** (seed ignored; random yaw removed): two tests were needed, not one. The first
  drives `board_frame_transform` directly across 20 seeds and checks the rotations aren't all
  identical — but that only proves `board_frame_transform` itself is correct, not that
  `generate_synthetic_sample` actually wires its own `seed` argument into the `rng` it builds. A
  hardcoded `default_rng(0)` inside `generate_synthetic_sample` passed the first test cleanly
  (confirmed by deliberately applying that exact mutation — nothing failed) since the first test
  never calls `generate_synthetic_sample` at all. The second test closes that gap by calling
  `generate_synthetic_sample` itself across 10 seeds and checking the sidecar's recorded rotation
  varies.
- **Base at z = 0** (a stray z-translation): output z bounds checked against the known fixture
  height with `sigma_mm=0` (exact, no noise to smear the bounds).
- **Sidecar σ**: checked against an explicit non-default value, not just a round-trip.

Also adds `_validate_base_at_zero`, raising `ValueError` when the input CAD's minimum z isn't 0 —
confirmed with the team over the AC's other option (recording it in the sidecar instead), matching
every other validation already in this module's strict style
(`_validate_mesh`/`densify_mesh`/`apply_gaussian_noise`/`select_patch_vertices` all raise on bad
input).

Mutation-tested all seven checks (the six from #199, plus the new validation) by hand: each
mutation fails exactly the test(s) built for it, nothing else — including the seed/yaw gap above,
found and closed during this same mutation-testing pass. Full suite 639 passed (was 633), ruff
