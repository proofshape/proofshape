## Why this exists

The photographs every accuracy result rests on live outside git, on Google Drive. Three committed
manifests point at them: `fixtures/golden_capture_manifest.json` (s-01 to s-05, F-06),
`specimen_capture_manifest.json` (G-08 and G-09) and `robustness_capture_manifest.json` (G-11).
Each records the Drive folder, the frame count and a content hash, and
`fixtures/download_golden_capture.sh` fetches and verifies them. So the code is already linked to
its data. Two things are wrong with where that data sits, checked on 2026-11-24:

- **The golden photos carry the GPS position they were taken at.** All 142 JPEGs of s-01 to s-05
  hold a GPS block in their EXIF. So do the 236 copies under `runs/r32-runs/` in the staging
  folder. The G-08 and G-11 photos carry none. The repository is public (D-013), its manifests
  name the folders, and the folders are shared as "anyone with the link". So anyone reading the
  repository can download that position.
- **Everything is owned by one student Google account.** The staging folder, `g-08/`, `g-11/` and
  `runs/` all are. If the account is closed after the semester, every manifest link breaks and
  nothing in the repository can be re-run against its photos.

The pipeline reads two EXIF fields itself: the 35 mm-equivalent focal length and the orientation
(`recon/board_pose.py`, `read_exif_fields`). So stripping all EXIF, or re-encoding the JPEGs,
would change calibration. Only the GPS block may go, and the image data must stay byte for byte.

## Acceptance criteria

- **A script removes the GPS block and nothing else.** `scripts/strip_capture_gps.py` writes
  copies to a new folder and never edits the originals. In each copy the GPS block is empty, and
  every other byte is unchanged: the image data, the focal length, the orientation, the other
  EXIF fields and the file's length. It needs no tool outside `requirements.txt`. A file without
  GPS is copied unchanged, and anything that isn't a JPEG is refused.
- **The golden set is replaced on Drive, and the manifest follows.** The stripped s-01 to s-05
  replace the originals in their existing Drive folders, so the folder IDs don't change. The old
  files are deleted rather than kept as versions. The manifest gets the new `content_sha256` and
  `aggregate_bytes`, and `golden_capture_manifest.sha256` is regenerated.
  `fixtures/download_golden_capture.sh` then passes from a clean folder, with the command and its
  output recorded here.
- **The other copies are found and dealt with.** `runs/r32-runs/` on Drive is replaced the same
  way. Every other copy the team controls is listed here and replaced or deleted: the Lightning
  Teamspace Drive (D-036), members' `fixtures/data/golden_capture/`, and saved session folders.
  A copy already downloaded by someone outside the team can't be recalled, and this story says
  so rather than claiming otherwise.
- **The photos get a home that outlives the semester.** The three manifests' photo sets, about
  1.8 GB, are deposited in one public, citable archive, with each set's content hash and the
  repository commit they match. Zenodo is the plan: it is free, keeps a record permanently and
  gives it a DOI. `fixtures/README.md` and the final report cite it. The downloader keeps
  reading Drive; pointing it at the archive is a later change if Drive goes away.
  - The archive also holds what the story records cite but the repository doesn't:
    `i15-placement-sheet.md` (I-15's readings), P-41's `experiment.py` (the screening runs R-44
    and I-15 compare against) and the G-01 and G-05 `.3mf` print files that still exist
    (D-085).
  - **Phone sessions stay out of the public archive** unless the person who took each one agrees.
    Some were taken on classmates' phones by people the team can't name (I-15's readings). The
    `runs/` folder's link sharing is narrowed to the team.

## Tests

`tests/test_strip_capture_gps.py`, synthetic, on JPEGs written with Pillow:
- with a GPS block, in both byte orders, the block comes out empty;
- the copy is the same length;
- every byte outside the GPS block's own entries and values is unchanged, so the image decodes
  identically;
- `read_exif_fields` returns the same focal length and orientation before and after;
- a JPEG without GPS is copied byte for byte;
- a non-JPEG is refused;
- the originals are untouched.

The Drive replacement and the archive are a written check, with commands and hashes in this file.
